Data & Analysis 3

Seattle 911 Response Times ↗

Seattle's 911 dispatch data promises officers reach Priority 1 calls in under 7 minutes. I cleaned 43,773 of 50,000 sampled calls — dropping test entries, bad timestamps, and outliers over 120 minutes — and found the North precinct alone missing that standard at a 7.2-minute median, while the rest of the city met it. A phase-2 logistic regression on 10,770 calls then predicted whether a Priority 1 call would clear in time from call type, geography, and time of day, scoring 0.723 ROC AUC on a chronological holdout against a 0.519 baseline.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • scikit-learn
  • Logistic regression
  • Open Data

Seattle Traffic Collision Analysis ↗

I live in Seattle, and I wanted to figure out where the city should actually be spending its safety budget. So I analyzed 221,266 traffic collision records to find out. Instead of just counting crashes, I built a scoring system that weighs how severe each one was, which showed that cyclist crashes along the Burke-Gilman Trail were a bigger problem than raw numbers suggested. I also checked whether missing streetlights were a cause, since that seemed like an easy fix, but the data showed they weren't.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • Matplotlib
  • Open Data

Hello! I'm Yuta, a B.S. Informatics student at the University of Washington on the Data Science track, expected to graduate in June 2029.

I'm drawn to the part of analysis that actually changes a decision. Volunteering at the University Food Bank, that meant working through distribution data to find which items kept running out — and cutting stockouts on them by 18%.
